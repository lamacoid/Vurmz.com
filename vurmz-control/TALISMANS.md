# VURMZ Talismans — the line (concept locked 2026-07-05/06, with Zach)

A line of engraved relic-talismans and matching lore cards, drawn from
the REAL geography and history of the south Denver metro, told in an
old-fantasy register. Elden Ring in feel, real ground underneath, names
held loosely in place. Each is a VURMZ Original: designed and made
in-house, buyable inventory (the "Originals" label finally gets stock).

## What it is, in one line

A carried talisman (layered wood + a set stone) plus an engraved
aluminum lore card. The talisman is the relic; the card is its scripture.

## The two tiers

- **Realms** — broad, recognizable places. Centennial, Littleton,
  Highlands Ranch, Aurora, Green Cherry Village (Greenwood Village +
  Cherry Hills, fused; the wealth realm).
- **Sites** — specific significant landmarks. A creek, a canal, a dam, a
  reservoir, the old mall. Some places are legendary enough to earn their
  own relic apart from their realm.

## The voice

Old-fantasy, FromSoftware item-description register. Accessible but
plainly not of this time (not of any time). Terse, poetic, a little
cryptic, wistful. Present-tense eternal statements. No modern diction.

The humor is EMERGENT from the gravitas, never jokey: the gravitas is
real, the subject is sometimes mundane, and the gap is the joke ("the
creek runs north, and no one asked it to"). Tonal range from heavy
(cursed, a cost, a sacrifice to transfer) to light (a free small
blessing). Buffs AND debuffs, always in words, NEVER numbers or points.

Relic-lore shape (old fantasy): what stone/part is bound to it, where it
was taken or found, the archetype it belonged to, how it was retrieved,
what it grants, what it costs, sometimes a price to pass it on.

## THE HARD RULES (Zach, non-negotiable)

1. **Never disparage a real person.** Boss-figures are mythic ARCHETYPES
   and titles (the King of Roots, the Dam-Keeper), never portraits of
   named real people given invented dark character. A real person appears
   ONLY in the plain factual credit on the card, and only honored.
2. **Nothing from Native-named things. At all.** No Arapahoe, no Smoky
   Hill (that trail's name and origin are indigenous — CUT), nothing of
   indigenous origin used as material. "I respect it too much to touch any
   of it." The whole set is settler-era, geographic, or mythological.
3. **Never invent history.** Every real-fact line on a card is verifiable
   and sourced. The fantasy is openly fantasy; the history is exactly true.
4. **Cultural references stay super-basic and harmless** (e.g. "GV is
   wealthy" as a broad class observation). Nothing deeper.

ancestry; published the TREC reports 2024). Alt: Denver Indian Center,
denverindiancenter.org.

## The physical object (MAKEABLE on Zach's current gear — he must really make these)

**The talisman (relic):**
- Ornate seal silhouette CUT from wood on the diode laser (Falcon 2). Not
  a rectangle: lobed disc, shield, teardrop, ringed frame.
- 2-3 wood layers stacked/glued for real relief: back plate, a PIERCED
  frame layer (cut-out tracery), an engraved face. Depth like the ER
  metal frames without any casting.
- Deep-engraved sigil / figure / rim text (blackletter: E780 or
  Charlemagne) on the face.
- A set stone: polished cabochon / tumbled stone / glass gem glued into a
  laser-cut recess. "The stone bound to this charm." Themeable per realm.
- Optional premium: a thin engraved aluminum ring/backplate on the FIBER
  laser (the precision he loves) for filigree.
- Aged finish: dark wash rubbed into the engraved recesses, sealed. A
  loop/jump ring to hang.
- Material note: use STABILIZED / resin-infused wood ("infused wood,"
  Zach's call) so it survives a keyring (raw slate chips; slate becomes a
  later "stone edition" sold as carry-gently; fiber-marked metal a later
  "reliquary edition").

**The lore card (scripture):** engraved on an ALUMINUM card (credit-card
size, his existing product, his fiber laser). Front: the talisman name +
the fantasy lore. Back or lower: the plain real-history line, honored and
sourced. Deliberately plain type for the true part — the costume comes off.

## Pricing (draft)

Talisman + aluminum lore card as a set. Blank + stone + card material is a
few dollars; the labor (cut, engrave, set, finish) is the real cost.
Target $18-24 each, or a "carry three" bundle. Premium metal-accent
edition higher. Confirm against real build time on the first prototypes.

## Monthly-special fit

Each new talisman is a ready-made monthly feature ("this month: the Ward
of the Broken Dam") + one GBP post + one IG post + a newsletter beat. The
line self-generates the marketing calendar.

# THE FIRST SET (drafted; every real fact sourced)

## Realms

### TALISMAN OF THE HUNDRED-YEAR CITY  (Centennial)
Struck into this seal is a hundred marks, one for each thousand who spoke
the city into being. It was not built stone by stone but willed whole, in
a single day, by a gathering the like of which had never been recorded.
- *Grants the strength of sudden multitudes, of many hands at once.*
- *It remembers it was made by agreement, and will not serve one who acts alone.*

(Centennial was incorporated in 2001 after a 2000 vote; ~100,000 residents
made it the largest incorporation in U.S. history at the time. Named for
the Centennial State — Colorado, the 38th, admitted 1876. Source:
en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centennial,_Colorado)

### SEAL OF LITTLE'S TOWN  (Littleton)
Bound here is a splinter of the first mill-wheel to turn on the great
river. An engineer came seeking gold, found little, and chose instead to
grind grain and dig the ditches that fed the valley. The town keeps his
small name still, though it is small no longer.
- *Grants patience to those who would build rather than dig.*
- *It has no love for the one who wants the fortune without the work.*

(Richard Sullivan Little, a New Hampshire engineer, arrived 1862, turned
from prospecting to founding the town; the Rough and Ready Mill and Ditch
Company came in 1867. Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littleton,_Colorado)

### TALISMAN OF THE KING OF ROOTS  (Highlands Ranch)
Bound to this charm is a knot of stabilized root, cut from the first field
broken on the high plain. They called its breaker a king, though he wore
no crown but the dirt under his nails. He turned hard ground to harvest
and asked the land for nothing it could not give. When the fields went
under the parcelling plow, a tender saved this root from the last unbuilt
corner.
- *Grants abundance to those who tend small and humble things.*
- *It asks only that you never mistake the harvest for your own doing.*

(Rufus "Potato" Clark, the real "Potato King of Colorado," homesteaded
here in 1859; the land, named for the Highland cattle raised on it, is now
the largest master-planned community in the state, 22,000 acres. Sources:
thehrhs.org/history, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highlands_Ranch_Mansion)

### TALISMAN OF THE SECOND DAWN  (Aurora)
Set in this charm is a sliver of the first brick of a town that drought
unmade. When the wells failed and the silver crashed, the survivors did
not mourn it. They took the name of the dawn-goddess and raised the town
again, as the light is raised each morning, asking no one's leave.
- *Grants the bearer one more beginning than they were owed.*
- *It does not grant the memory of the first.*

(Incorporated as Fletcher in 1891; a drought and the Silver Panic of 1893
nearly ended it; renamed Aurora in 1907 for the Roman goddess of the dawn.
Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora,_Colorado)

### TALISMAN OF THE GILDED VALE  (Green Cherry Village — Greenwood Village + Cherry Hills)
In the vale of Green Cherry the lawns are always cut, by hands you will
never see, and the gates stand open, for nothing here has needed locking.
Fortune comes to this place, and most of it was already here.
- *Grants good fortune, most of it inherited.*
- *It will not teach the bearer how it was earned, for it does not know.*

(Cherry Hills Village is the wealthiest municipality in Colorado. Its
country club hosted the 1960 U.S. Open, where Arnold Palmer erased a
seven-stroke deficit in a single afternoon — the greatest comeback of its
age, in perhaps the one place whose people have never needed one. Sources:
en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_Hills_Village,_Colorado, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_U.S._Open_(golf))

## Sites

### CHARM OF THE FIRST GOLD  (Cherry Creek / Little Dry Creek)
Twenty flecks of light are sealed within — not the true gold, but struck
in its memory. Where a small dry creek spills onto the plain, a wanderer
drew the first real gold of these mountains from the water, and the telling
of it turned a hundred thousand faces toward the sunset. Most who followed
found nothing. He was gone before they came.
- *Grants sudden fortune to the patient sifter.*
- *Grants also the restless hunger that emptied the East.*

(In July 1858 William Green Russell's party found the first significant
gold in the Rockies — about 20 troy ounces — near the mouth of Little Dry
Creek, which rises in Centennial. It set off the Pike's Peak Gold Rush.
Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Dry_Creek_(Englewood,_Colorado))

### CHARM OF THE NORTH-FLOWING CREEK  (Cherry Creek) — light, no cost
A vein of the creekbed is bound here. Of all the waters of the plain, this
one alone refuses the common direction and runs against it, north and
northward, longer than any name laid upon it.
- *Grants the stubbornness to hold your own course when the world runs the other.*
- *(no cost. Some blessings are simply given.)*

(Cherry Creek flows north through the metro to join the South Platte in
Denver. Source: general geography; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_Creek_(Colorado))

### SEAL OF THE HIGH LINE  (the High Line Canal)
Bound here is a grain of the canal's bed. It was dug by shovel and donkey
in a single year, and made to fall but two feet in every mile, so the
water is sent the long way round — arriving late, but arriving still.
- *Grants slow, certain progress to those with far to go.*
- *It has no gift for the one in a hurry.*

(The High Line Canal, completed 1883, runs 71 miles by gravity alone,
dropping about two feet per mile; dug by hand for ~$650,000. Sources:
historycolorado.org, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Line_Canal)

### WARD OF THE BROKEN DAM  (Castlewood) — heavy
A shard of old masonry is set in this ward, quarried from the wall that
failed. The Dam-Keeper heard it go in the dark hour before morning, and a
voice went out along the wire, hand to hand, until the low places emptied.
The wall took two souls and might have taken five thousand. The stone
remembers which it was.
- *Wards the bearer against their own certainty.*
- *It will not ward against the thing they never saw coming.*

(Castlewood Canyon Dam failed at ~1 a.m. on August 3, 1933, sending an
eleven-foot wall of water down Cherry Creek; phone warnings passed person
to person saved an estimated 5,000. Two died. Sources:
history.denverlibrary.org, damfailures.org)

### TALISMAN OF THE HALL REOPENED  (the Southglenn mall) — light
A chip of the old roof is bound here. For a generation the people gathered
beneath one great roof; then the roof came down, and they gathered still,
beneath the open sky, in the same place, as if nothing had ended.
- *Grants renewal to things thought finished.*
- *It quietly reminds the bearer that not all endings are endings.*

(Southglenn Mall opened enclosed in 1974, closed in 2006, and reopened in
2009 as the open-air Streets at SouthGlenn. Source:
en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Streets_at_SouthGlenn)

### CHARM OF THE WILLOW CREEK  (Willow Creek / "Creek of the Willows") — light
A withe of willow is bound to this charm, taken from the creek that gave
the willows their standing. Along its slow water the willows have always
leaned, and the water has always let them.
- *Grants the art of bending without breaking.*
- *(no cost.)*

(Willow Creek and its neighborhood take their name from the willows along
the waterway. Source: local geography; homes.com/local-guide/centennial-co/willow-creek-neighborhood)
