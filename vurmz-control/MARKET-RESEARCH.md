## TL;DR — what this changes about strategy
1. **Transparent posted pricing is your moat.** All three nearby competitors
   researched (Alpine Trophies, Lantern Engraving, Mile High Laser) hide their
   prices behind quote forms. You publish $35/$25 on the site. Lead with it.
2. **Speed is your second moat.** The Denver competitor's default is 3–5 days;
   same-day costs a rush fee, in-store only [verified]. Your next-day +
   hand-delivery default beats their paid tier.
3. **Your core niches look uncontested locally.** The Littleton incumbent sells
   awards/trophies; it lists no knife engraving, service tags, metal cards, or
   pens at all [sourced].
4. **One pricing gap: knife volume.** A national mail-in shop charges $35 for
   one knife (you win at $25) but $10–15/knife at 4–6+ [sourced]. For
   restaurant crew pickups, consider a crew tier (e.g. 4+ knives → ~$18–20/ea)
   so the math beats mailing a knife roll to a website. Your call — I changed
   nothing.
5. **Q4 is the whole game for B2C.** Start holiday prep in August, listings and
   marketing live by October. It is June — put it on the calendar now.
6. **The $10k+/month solo shops get there on repeat B2B** (corporate, wholesale,
   trades), not one-off gifts [sourced]. That's exactly what LEADS.md is for.
