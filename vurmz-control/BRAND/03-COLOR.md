# VURMZ Brand Binder
## 03 · Color

> Approved direction, 2026-06-21: "paper with teal ink." Oatmeal is the page, teal is the ink and the dark feature blocks, coral is action, red is the laser spark. Plain language, no em-dashes.

### The palette (five roles, that is the whole system)

**Oatmeal (paper).** `#DED6C3`, rgb(222, 214, 195).
The dominant surface. Default page background and light sections. The "paper" everything sits on.

**Deep teal (ink and anchor).** `#16525C`, rgb(22, 82, 92).
Primary text and headings on the paper. Also the header, the footer, and the standard dark feature sections.

**Deepest teal (feature).** `#123F47`, rgb(18, 63, 71).
The darkest feature bands, for depth and contrast against the paper.

**Glassy teal (cool accent).** `#7FCFD4`, rgb(127, 207, 212).
Accents ON the dark teal sections only: eyebrows, links, icons, the backlit glow. Too light to read on paper, so never use it there.

**Dusty coral (action).** `#C67A6F`, rgb(198, 122, 111). Hover `#B0675D`. Active `#99584F`.
CTAs and key actions. Always a fill with white text. Not for body text on paper (contrast is too low).

**Laser red (spark).** `#FF2A2A`.
The laser motif only: the cursor and small precision marks. Never decorative, never a background.

### The ratio (target)
- Oatmeal paper: about 55 percent
- Teal, ink plus dark sections: about 30 percent
- Coral: about 12 percent
- Laser red: about 3 percent

A visitor scrolls mostly warm paper, punctuated by deep teal feature blocks, with coral only where you want a click.

### Placement rules
- Default page background: oatmeal paper.
- Body and headings on paper: deep teal. Secondary text: a muted teal grey.
- Feature sections (hero, key callouts, footer): deep or deepest teal, with oatmeal or white text and glassy teal accents.
- Buttons and CTAs: coral fill, white text. Used sparingly.
- Links on paper: deep teal or coral. Links on dark teal: glassy teal.
- Laser red: cursor and small precision marks only.

### Do and do not
- Do let the paper breathe. White space reads warm here, not empty.
- Do keep coral rare, so it still means "click."
- Do not put glassy teal text on paper. It disappears.
- Do not use coral as body text on paper. Use it as a fill.
- Do not add a sixth color. Five roles is the system.
- Do not go back to all teal.

### Contrast (checked)
- Deep teal `#16525C` on oatmeal `#DED6C3`: about 6.6 to 1. Good for text.
- Coral as a fill with white text: good. Coral as text on oatmeal: too low, avoid.
